Retail Lab · Agent Experiment

Agent-Based Search

Marketplace filters trust the spec table. An agent doesn't — it reads the spec table, then goes and checks whether the spec table is lying. This experiment runs one real, deliberately hard shopping request end to end and shows the difference.

Why this is a different kind of search

Keyword and filter search on Amazon/Wayfair/Walmart answers "what's listed as matching?" Agent-based search answers "what actually matches, and how do I know?" — by treating every retailer spec field as a claim to verify, not a fact to filter on.

Marketplace filter search
  • Trusts the "Product Dimensions" field as-is
  • Can't tell an overall measurement from an interior one
  • Has no way to catch a scrambled W×H×D field
  • Returns everything that matches the filter, verified or not
Agent-based search
  • Treats the listed spec as a hypothesis, not an answer
  • Hunts for an independent source — dimension diagram, assembly PDF, cut sheet, buyer review — before trusting an interior measurement
  • Separates verified results from inferred ones, and shows the arithmetic behind every inference
  • Actively looks for the specific failure mode that burned the requester before, in every candidate

Example prompt

This is the actual request run through the agent for this experiment — a buffet/sideboard/credenza search with a binding interior-dimension requirement, and an explicit warning about a prior bad purchase caused by a scrambled spec field.

I need to find a buffet/sideboard/credenza. Hard requirements: RETAILER (scope restriction): - Amazon, IKEA, or Wayfair only. No Amish/solid-wood makers, office furniture dealers, or vintage dealers this pass — just these three. DIMENSIONS (interior measurements are the binding constraint): - Interior cabinet compartment: minimum 25" WIDE and minimum 14" DEEP. These are INSIDE-the-cabinet usable dimensions, not overall furniture dimensions. - Overall depth will therefore need to be ~17-20" (carcass walls and door thickness typically eat 3-4" of depth and ~3" of width). - Total wall run: 120" maximum, OUTSIDE dimension, across a combination of matching-style base cabinets at various widths — not limited to two identical units. - Buffet/counter height preferred: roughly 32-36" tall. - Must be generally available to buy now. Temporarily out of stock is fine. Amazon's "Currently unavailable. We don't know when or if this item will be back in stock." reads as discontinued, not a short restock wait — disqualify regardless of how well it otherwise passed. - Color: black or dark grey only. Check the product line itself for a black/dark-grey variant rather than assuming one exists — if a line only ships in one finish and it isn't black/dark-grey, it's disqualified even if every dimension passes. PRIMARY CRITERIA — maximize total storage units, not just pass/fail: - Count how many 4.4"W x 14"D x 4.4"H storage units fit inside the combination. A bay contributes floor(width/4.4) width-multiples times floor(depth/14) times floor(interior-clear-height/4.4). Sum this across every cabinet in the combination; that total is the number to maximize within the 120" run, not the count of cabinets or the count of qualifying bays. STORAGE TYPE: - Cabinet space with adjustable shelves is the priority. Drawers are a negative — zero drawers preferred, minimal drawers acceptable. - A 25"+ wide bay means the piece must NOT have a center divider behind each door pair. Typical 4-door/3-divider layouts yield ~14" bays and FAIL. Two-door cabinets with a single full-width adjustable shelf usually have no divider and PASS if wide enough. CRITICAL METHODOLOGY — READ THIS: Retailer spec tables in this product category are frequently WRONG, and marketplace filter facets inherit those errors. I have already been burned: a cabinet listed and filtered as 18" deep is actually 11.8" deep with a 10" interior shelf. Its spec field read "8"W x 14"H x 18"D" — axes scrambled. So do NOT trust: - Amazon/Wayfair/IKEA depth or dimension filter facets - The "Product Dimensions" field in a marketplace spec table - Marketing copy adjectives like "deep" or "spacious" DO verify each candidate against at least one of: - A manufacturer/IKEA dimension diagram image showing interior measurements - An assembly instruction PDF (IKEA publishes these for nearly every product, usually with interior callouts) - The named brand's own spec sheet, where Amazon/Wayfair source from one - Customer Q&A or review text where someone reports measuring the interior - Cross-checking the same SKU across 2+ of these three retailers for dimensional agreement OUTPUT FORMAT: A table with one row per candidate: product+brand, overall W×D×H, interior bay W×D, doors/drawers, price, retailer+link, and interior dims labelled PUBLISHED or INFERRED. Unverified candidates go in a separate section. Prioritize PUBLISHED. 3 verified options beat 15 guesses.

Restricting the search to just these three retailers made the category noticeably harder — most of what's sold there is flat-pack and shallower than the 14" interior floor — but 1 candidate currently passes every requirement including color, on IKEA, with 2 more runner-ups. Adding a black/dark-grey requirement cost this page its previous #1 pick: TONSTAD Cabinet with Doors was the cleanest dimensional match found, but it only ships in off-white — checked the line directly, no black or dark-grey variant exists, so it's disqualified on color alone (see Ruled Out). Working combinations — stacking multiple cabinets to fill the 120" run

A single cabinet is one candidate; a family like HAVSTA — which sells more than one width in the same finish — supports several different end-to-end stackings. Each combination below only uses candidates with zero deficient criteria (a unit that already fails on its own, like VIHALS' height or the Prepac console's depth, can't be fixed by combining it with another), sums their 4.4"×14×4.4" storage-slot counts, and shows the literal width arithmetic that keeps the total at or under the 120" exterior run. Every cabinet name links to its listing. Only combinations that are practically different are shown: the max-slot plan a family can fit, plus an alternate only when swapping in a different model actually changes the cost/verification trade-off or gets you to zero unverified risk — not "3 fit, here's 2 and 1 too."

How the verification actually worked

  1. Even inside a fixed retailer scope, hunt for the exceptions — within just Amazon/IKEA/Wayfair, most listings are shallow flat-pack, but a handful (IKEA's TONSTAD/HAVSTA lines, Amazon's Costzon buffet) run deep enough to pass. Restricting scope doesn't mean lowering the bar on verification.
  2. For every promising candidate, look past the marketplace spec table for an independent source: IKEA's own measurement diagrams, an Amazon listing's in-image dimension infographic, or a named brand's spec sheet where Amazon/Wayfair source from one.
  3. Sanity-check the numbers against each other, not just against the requirement — e.g. does overall height minus interior height leave a plausible amount for the base/top/drawers, or does the ratio look scrambled the way the prior bad purchase was?
  4. Actively hunt for the specific failure mode named in the prompt (a center divider hiding behind a wide-looking cabinet) in every candidate — which is exactly what surfaced IKEA's TONSTAD Sideboard as a divided-bay trap sharing a product family name with TONSTAD Cabinet with Doors, itself a passing cabinet later disqualified separately on color.
  5. Separate what's confirmed from what's inferred, and show the arithmetic for every inference, rather than presenting a best-guess number with the same confidence as a published one.
  6. Say plainly where coverage is thin — Wayfair's own pages rate-limited every direct fetch this pass, so its results lean on secondary sources; that gap is reported, not papered over with an unsupported "no results" or a false pass.
