Retail Lab · Agent Experiment

Agent-Based Search

Marketplace filters trust the spec table. An agent doesn't — it reads the spec table, then goes and checks whether the spec table is lying. This experiment runs one real, deliberately hard shopping request end to end and shows the difference.

Why this is a different kind of search

Keyword and filter search on Amazon/Wayfair/Walmart answers "what's listed as matching?" Agent-based search answers "what actually matches, and how do I know?" — by treating every retailer spec field as a claim to verify, not a fact to filter on.

Marketplace filter search
  • Trusts the "Product Dimensions" field as-is
  • Can't tell an overall measurement from an interior one
  • Has no way to catch a scrambled W×H×D field
  • Returns everything that matches the filter, verified or not
Agent-based search
  • Treats the listed spec as a hypothesis, not an answer
  • Hunts for an independent source — dimension diagram, assembly PDF, cut sheet, buyer review — before trusting an interior measurement
  • Separates verified results from inferred ones, and shows the arithmetic behind every inference
  • Actively looks for the specific failure mode that burned the requester before, in every candidate

Example prompt

This is the actual request run through the agent for this experiment — a buffet/sideboard/credenza search with a binding interior-dimension requirement, and an explicit warning about a prior bad purchase caused by a scrambled spec field.

I need to find a buffet/sideboard/credenza. Hard requirements: RETAILER (scope restriction): - Amazon, IKEA, or Wayfair only. No Amish/solid-wood makers, office furniture dealers, or vintage dealers this pass — just these three. DIMENSIONS (interior measurements are the binding constraint): - Interior cabinet compartment: minimum 25" WIDE and minimum 14" DEEP. These are INSIDE-the-cabinet usable dimensions, not overall furniture dimensions. - Overall depth will therefore need to be ~17-20" (carcass walls and door thickness typically eat 3-4" of depth and ~3" of width). - Total wall run: 120" maximum. Either one single unit, or two units in a matching style placed side by side, each 60" or less. - Buffet/counter height preferred: roughly 32-36" tall. STORAGE TYPE: - Cabinet space with adjustable shelves is the priority. Drawers are a negative — zero drawers preferred, minimal drawers acceptable. - A 25"+ wide bay means the piece must NOT have a center divider behind each door pair. Typical 4-door/3-divider layouts yield ~14" bays and FAIL. Two-door cabinets with a single full-width adjustable shelf usually have no divider and PASS if wide enough. CRITICAL METHODOLOGY — READ THIS: Retailer spec tables in this product category are frequently WRONG, and marketplace filter facets inherit those errors. I have already been burned: a cabinet listed and filtered as 18" deep is actually 11.8" deep with a 10" interior shelf. Its spec field read "8"W x 14"H x 18"D" — axes scrambled. So do NOT trust: - Amazon/Wayfair/IKEA depth or dimension filter facets - The "Product Dimensions" field in a marketplace spec table - Marketing copy adjectives like "deep" or "spacious" DO verify each candidate against at least one of: - A manufacturer/IKEA dimension diagram image showing interior measurements - An assembly instruction PDF (IKEA publishes these for nearly every product, usually with interior callouts) - The named brand's own spec sheet, where Amazon/Wayfair source from one - Customer Q&A or review text where someone reports measuring the interior - Cross-checking the same SKU across 2+ of these three retailers for dimensional agreement OUTPUT FORMAT: A table with one row per candidate: product+brand, overall W×D×H, interior bay W×D, doors/drawers, price, retailer+link, and interior dims labelled PUBLISHED or INFERRED. Unverified candidates go in a separate section. Prioritize PUBLISHED. 3 verified options beat 15 guesses.

DutchCrafters publishes a distinct "Inside Door Space Dimensions" spec line (28"H × 51⅜"W × 17½"D) separate from the overall measurement — exactly the independent interior figure the prompt required. The three door faces total only 45⅜" combined, narrower than the 51⅜" interior cavity, which only makes sense if the face-frame mullions are cosmetic and the box behind them is open corner-to-corner — consistent with the listing's "one adjustable shelf" (singular) spanning the full width. Height math checks out too: 35.25" overall − 28" interior leaves a sane 7.25" for the drawer bank, top, and base — not the kind of scrambled ratio that caused the prior bad purchase.

Ruled out in this pass

  • HON Brigade steel storage cabinets — clears depth easily but overall height (41¾") runs well above the 32-36" band; HON's shorter "credenza height" line instead sits around 29-30", too short. Office steel storage seems to cluster on either side of the buffet-height band with a gap in the middle.
  • Stock IKEA flat-pack sideboards (BJURSTA, STOCKHOLM, FJÄLLBO, HEMNES, SKRUVBY) — where an overall depth was published at all, it landed around 16.5", which the prompt's own carcass-thickness math predicts fails the 14" interior floor. Not pursued to individual SKU level.
  • IKEA SEKTION base-cabinet-as-sideboard build — the specific frame checked (single door, 24"×24"×30") is both too narrow and too short as a standalone SKU; a wider double-door SEKTION frame remains a plausible DIY lead but needs its own assembly PDF checked for a center support before it's worth listing.

How the verification actually worked

  1. Search broadly across categories biased toward real depth — office/commercial credenzas, Amish/solid-wood makers, vintage/mid-century dealers — rather than generic flat-pack listings, which the prompt correctly predicted would mostly fail on interior depth.
  2. For every promising candidate, look past the marketplace spec table for an independent source: a manufacturer's own "interior" spec line, an assembly PDF, a cut sheet, or buyer-reported measurements.
  3. Sanity-check the numbers against each other, not just against the requirement — e.g. does overall height minus interior height leave a plausible amount for the base/top/drawers, or does the ratio look scrambled the way the prior bad purchase was?
  4. Actively hunt for the specific failure mode named in the prompt (a center divider hiding behind a wide-looking cabinet) in every candidate — which is exactly what surfaced the Bunker Hill Sideboard trap sitting right next to the winning candidate.
  5. Separate what's confirmed from what's inferred, and show the arithmetic for every inference, rather than presenting a best-guess number with the same confidence as a published one.
