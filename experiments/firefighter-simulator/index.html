    Source note

    What this page is

    Modern 3D reconstruction inspired by the documented predecessor; not original software or validated firefighting training.

    Hughes Associates, Inc. — Christina Uyak, Tom Hughes, Jerry Back, and Wade Grimm — prepared Situation Simulator for Firefighters, ESL-TR-86-35, October 1988, for the Air Force Engineering and Services Center at Tyndall AFB. The work covered December 1984 through March 1986. The public scan is DTIC AD-A221132 on archive.org. Appendix A is the operating manual.

    Documented, and modeled here as toy rules

    • Two courses: an exterior engine fire on the ramp, and an interior fire after landing with people still aboard.
    • Units from the manual: Crash 3 / P-15, Crash 4 / P-2, Crash 5 / P-2, Rescue 6 / P-10, Engine 7 structural, Crash 9 / P-13. Position them before firefighting. Hydrant, modulate, and stand-by are separate modes.
    • Activities: size-up, command, control, extinguish, protect, rescue, vent, overhaul, and a game clock. Command includes secure aircraft, communicate, request assistance, and return units.
    • Assistance: off-duty personnel, aircraft maintenance, disaster control, SCBA air resupply, mutual aid fire, foam resupply, tech services.
    • Tools: turret, handline, penetrator, portable extinguisher. Agents: AFFF, dry chemical, Halon, water. Pattern is straight stream or fog.
    • The 1988 program checked agent quantity and flow, whether the vehicle was close enough for the stream to reach, ventilation, smoke, and rescue timing. Using Halon before rescue, in the interior scenario, increased the death rate. Here that becomes an abstract unaccounted count.
    • Wind and temperature were displayed and did not affect the outcome. An after-action printout listed decisions, time, and fire size.

    What the original showed

    Photographs of a C-141B with animated smoke and flame, played from a laserdisc on an NCR InteracTV-2 touchscreen, plus computer text and menus. The disc held about 30 minutes of audio and video on a side. It was not a real-time 3D scene. No copy of that disc was used here.

    Invented for this page

    The procedural aircraft, trucks, tarmac, flame, smoke, and spray are a modern WebGL picture. So are the orbit, map, bumper, and interior cameras, and the guided demo. Every distance, flow, and “square foot” on the board is a made-up index.

    The scenario library adds fictional aircraft and incidents: a turboprop, a large tanker, a business jet, single- and twin-engine fighters, and a helicopter, on an apron, a runway, beside a hangar, at night, or on a practice deck. Those are not in the 1988 manual, not historical Hughes software, and not evidence about later work. Night lighting does not change the fire. A few incidents state an extra toy assumption in the brief.

    This is not the 1984–86 software, not any later simulator, and not a fielded Air Force or Navy system. It is not a fire model and not tactical instruction. Do not use it as a procedure.
