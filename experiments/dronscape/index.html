Feature Landscape

Dronscape

A capability taxonomy for combat drones, organized by what a feature actually does — human/AI control balance, fleet coordination, hardware, comms, regulation — rather than by which company or program described it. Collapsed, this is just a feature list; expand any item for its detail, source, and (where available) timestamp.

Company-agnostic by design: the categories and spectrum tiers below hold features from multiple programs. Human/AI-Hybrid Control and Fleet & Swarm Capabilities are framed as spectrums with tier ladders; most tiers now have a real sourced example, one still doesn't (marked "Concept") — that's the taxonomy's own gap, not a claim that no one has built it.
Two source tiers, not one: items tagged Neros · ... come from three full-transcript interviews with Neros CTO Olaf Hichwa (Oct 2024 – Aug 2026) — high confidence in wording, and a growing number of items are now corroborated across two or three of those interviews rather than mentioned once. Items badged Research come from a separate, meaningfully less-verified batch — a research/industry-literature compilation built from search-result snippets, not fetched source pages, and not independently checked here. See "Sourcing & methodology" at the bottom for exactly what that means before treating either batch as ground truth.

Human/AI-Hybrid Control

11 sourced · 1 conceptual

A spectrum from full manual piloting to full machine autonomy — where the human stays in the loop, where they physically can't be, where they delegate to a whole swarm at once, and where the industry hasn't gone (yet, at least not openly).

TIER 1Assisted Flight, Human-Primary
Autopilot/AI reduces workload; the human still flies and decides
Operator Fatigue Reduction Neros · Archer Live
Minimizes operator workload during 25–30 minute transit flights and high-workload terminal strikes.
▶ 08:03
TIER 2Supervisory Swarm Tasking (Human-on-the-Loop)
The human sets mission goals and rules of engagement for a whole swarm; the swarm handles low-level path planning and execution
High-Level Objective Tasking Defence Industries Research
Operators define high-level mission parameters, rules of engagement (ROE), and priority target decks, while the swarm autonomously handles low-level path planning, deconfliction, and execution. (Defence Industries, 2026)
Immersive & Multimodal Human-Swarm Interfaces (HSI) DARPA OFFSET Research
Command interfaces using AR, VR, touch, gesture, and voice controls to monitor and maneuver hundreds of autonomous nodes simultaneously. (DARPA OFFSET, 2016)
Tactical "Swarm Grammar" & Dynamic Playbooks DARPA OFFSET Research
Modular, parameterized tactic building blocks — similar to sporting playbooks — that let operators design or freestyle multi-agent behaviors in real time. (DARPA OFFSET, 2016)
Variable Levels of Interoperability (LOI) BAE Systems Research
Flexible control hierarchies letting a manned lead asset (airborne or ground) direct autonomous wingmen, pull sensor streams, or delegate kinetic payloads directly from a tactical display — the delegation level itself is adjustable per mission, not fixed. (BAE Systems, 2026)
TIER 3Supervised Autonomy, Live Override
AI executes a complex sub-task (e.g. terminal guidance); human retains override/veto authority whenever the link is live
Computer Vision Terminal Guidance Neros · Archer Live
Front-facing optical computer vision and AI target locking that autonomously flies the drone into the designated target.
▶ 09:44
Live Operator Override & Retargeting Neros · Archer Live
Real-time human-in-the-loop adjustments to correct or retarget the AI lock box when communication is active.
▶ 10:03
Human-in-the-Loop Lethal Action Verification Defence Industries Research
Fail-safe architecture requiring human operator authorization for weapons release and real-time bounding-box retargeting, complying with ROE norms. This is close to a near-universal operational requirement across fielded lethal systems generally, not a novel or unusual design choice — included here as the clearest concrete example of this tier's defining property. (Defence Industries, 2026)
TIER 4Contested-Link Autonomy
AI takes over fully during comms-denied/degraded windows, because a human literally cannot be in the loop there
RF Horizon / BLOS Dive Neros · Archer Live
Terminal guidance operates autonomously after diving below the radio horizon, through trees, or into dead zones. Corroborated across two separate interviews, described the same way roughly a year and a half apart.
▶ 10:47 ▶ 09:50 ▶ 1:06:59 (Ep #60)
GPS-Denied Operation Neros · Archer Live
Maintains strike accuracy without active GNSS/satellite signals.
▶ 10:21
TIER 5Autonomous Tactical Sub-Tasking
AI independently computes and executes a full tactical sub-problem within a human-set mission, no per-step input
Autonomous Intercept Trajectory Computation Neros · Bandit Future
High-autonomy path intercept algorithms optimized for aerial target geometry ("dot in the sky").
▶ 18:48
TIER 6Full Autonomous Engagement
The AI would make the terminal engage/no-engage decision itself, with no human confirmation at any point
Autonomous Engagement Decision Concept
Included to mark where this spectrum would end, not because any source here describes it as implemented — the most legally and ethically contested tier. Notably, the Tier 3 "Human-in-the-Loop Lethal Action Verification" item is sourced material actively describing the opposite design choice (mandatory human authorization), which is real evidence this tier is being deliberately avoided in public program descriptions, not just unmentioned.

Fleet & Swarm Capabilities

9 sourced

A spectrum from one operator flying one aircraft to fully decentralized multi-drone coordination. Every tier now has at least one sourced example — the two upper tiers were placeholders until a broader research/industry-literature pass filled them in.

TIER 1One-to-Many Command & Control
A single ground station/operator manages multiple simultaneous radio links
Multi-Radio Management Interface Neros · Archer Live
Ground station UI supporting automated and manual mode switching across six distinct radios.
▶ 33:20
High-Altitude Autonomous Aerial Dispenser Neros · Program Future
Dropping modular FPVs from high-altitude autonomous mother-ships, with mid-air stabilization, power-on sequencing, and terminal dispersion — one mothership managing many subordinate units is the same "one-to-many" shape as multi-radio ground control, just airborne.
▶ 15:48 (Ep #2) ▶ 16:53 (Ep #2)
TIER 2Swarm-Scale Defense (Many-vs-Many)
Mass-produced interceptors fielded to match the volume of an incoming drone raid — the swarm is on the attacking side; defense is unit economics at scale
Kinetic Counter-UAS Defense Neros · Bandit Future
Drone-on-drone interceptor platform designed for base defense against loitering munitions such as Shahed-136 — defending against a swarm-scale raid requires fielding interceptors at comparable scale, which is what Bandit's shared component architecture (see Hardware) is built to enable.
▶ 16:15 ▶ 54:32 (Ep #60)
Autonomous Border Shield / Drone Wall Architecture Neros · Program Future
Networked border-launch and recharge stations providing persistent, autonomous kinetic perimeter defense — a distributed defense grid rather than Bandit's single-interceptor-vs-single-threat framing above.
▶ 49:02 (Ep #2)
TIER 3Coordinated Multi-Drone Tasking
Multiple drones share targets/sensor data and deconflict tasking among themselves, rather than each being independently piloted
"Queen Bee" Multi-Drone Collaborative Strike Neros · Program Future
Single-operator supervisory control managing a localized cluster of semi-autonomous wingman drones (a "cloud of bombs") executing coordinated multi-target strikes — the first named, Neros-specific example at this tier, rather than a general industry description.
▶ 56:00 (Ep #60)
Dynamic Decentralized Task Allocation arXiv Research
Autonomous negotiation across peer nodes for task assignments (area coverage, tracking, kinetic engagement) without reliance on a single vulnerable command node. (arXiv:2601.14437, 2026)
Distributed Perception & Multi-Sensor Fusion Mercury Systems Research
Collective sensing combining EO/IR, RF telemetry, and acoustic inputs across multiple platforms into a single coherent tactical operating picture. (Mercury Systems, 2026; Defence Industries, 2026)
TIER 4Autonomous Swarm Behavior
Decentralized, self-organizing multi-drone coordination with no per-unit human controller
Collective Collision Avoidance & Formation Flocking Unmanned Systems Tech Research
Biomimetic flocking algorithms that adjust inter-agent spacing, dynamically routing around obstacles, terrain hazards, and air defense corridors. (Unmanned Systems Technology, 2026)
TIER 5Heterogeneous Multi-Domain Teaming
Coordination extends beyond same-type drone swarms to mixed platform classes across domains
Heterogeneous Teaming (Air-to-Ground / Multi-Tiered) DARPA OFFSET Research
Coordinated operation between varying drone classes — high-altitude ISR platforms, attritable small strike UAVs (sUAS), and unmanned ground systems (UGS). (DARPA OFFSET, 2016; Defence Industries, 2026)

Edge AI, Computer Vision & Terminal Guidance

4 sourced

The onboard perception and control tech that makes the autonomy tiers above possible — described here at the general industry-capability level, distinct from any one company's specific implementation.

Hybrid Computer Vision Pipeline Neros · Archer Live
Merges lightweight classical computer vision ("Trad ML") with edge deep learning to optimize object tracking within strict size/weight/power (SWaP) limits — a concrete, named counterpart to the general "Onboard Edge Inference" item below.
▶ 1:00:06 (Ep #60)
Onboard Edge Inference Defence Industries Research
Lightweight machine learning models hosted locally on each airframe for real-time target identification, classification, and anomaly detection, without relying on cloud or offboard compute. (Defence Industries, 2026)
Autonomous Terminal Homing & BLOS Dives Defence Industries Research
Optical AI tracking capable of locking onto a designated target and autonomously guiding the vehicle to impact even after dipping below terrain/RF line-of-sight — the same capability described concretely on Neros Archer as "RF Horizon / BLOS Dive" (see Human/AI-Hybrid Control, Tier 4), stated here as a general industry capability rather than one company's implementation. (Defence Industries, 2026)
Adaptive Learned Dynamics & Damage Compensation Unmanned Systems Tech Research
Flight controllers running learned dynamics models that adapt in real time to severe weather, wind turbulence, and physical airframe/motor damage. (Unmanned Systems Technology, 2026)

Resilient Comms & Contested/Degraded Environments (EW/A2AD)

4 sourced

How an entire swarm — not just one drone — keeps functioning when links are jammed, GPS is denied, or bandwidth is scarce. A different scope than the single-drone comms above: this is about the mesh, not the individual link.

Bent-Pipe Airborne RF Relay Integration Neros · Archer Live
Coordinated airborne repeaters providing over-the-horizon RF relay links into subterranean bunkers and trenches below terrain line-of-sight — multiple aircraft relaying for each other, not a single drone's own radio.
▶ 1:06:37 (Ep #60)
Self-Healing Mobile Ad-Hoc Mesh Networking (MANET) Fraunhofer IIS Research
Decentralized peer-to-peer data links where information hops across adjacent nodes via heartbeat broadcasts, keeping the swarm coordinated even when central links are severed. (Fraunhofer IIS, 2026; arXiv:2601.14437, 2026)
GPS/GNSS-Denied Navigation Mercury Systems Research
Relative visual-inertial odometry, optical flow, and collaborative localization among swarm members to maintain navigation accuracy under heavy electronic warfare jamming — a swarm-collaborative mechanism, distinct from Neros Archer's single-drone "GPS-Denied Operation" (see Human/AI-Hybrid Control, Tier 4), which doesn't rely on other drones being present. (Mercury Systems, 2026; Defence Industries, 2026)
Asynchronous Message-Passing & Bandwidth Throttling Mercury Systems Research
Prioritizing and batching critical telemetry and threat data so the network functions smoothly across constrained, low-power, or interrupted data links. (Mercury Systems, 2026; arXiv:2601.14437, 2026)

Sourcing & methodology
This page draws on two batches with meaningfully different confidence levels — read this before treating either as ground truth.

Batch 1 — items tagged "Neros · ...": from three full-transcript interviews with Neros CTO Olaf Hichwa, each retrieved via its own youtube:get_video_information tool call (full transcripts, not snippets):
  • The Drone Ultimatum, Ep #2 — "Drone Swarms & The New Tech of Warfare" (Oct 2024)
  • The Drone Ultimatum, Ep #60 — "Building American Drones in the New Age of Drone Warfare" (Jan 2026)
  • Relentless Podcast — "Manufacturing 1 Million Drones a Year" (Aug 2026)
"Live"/"Future" badges reflect how each feature was framed in its interview at the time of recording, not a live product-status feed. Timestamps not labeled with an episode number are from the Relentless Podcast source; "(Ep #2)" and "(Ep #60)" mark the two Drone Ultimatum episodes. Some figures across interviews are close-but-not-identical (e.g. a poka-yoke assembly timestamp cited as both 42:22 and 42:34) — small transcription/citation variance around the same real moment, not two different claims. One figure is a genuine distinct claim, not variance: "130,000+ drones/year run-rate" (Automated Manufacturing Scaling, Hardware category) and "1 million drones/year by ~2028" (the page's recurring manufacturing-goal framing) are two different numbers from the same interviews — a nearer-term run-rate target and a longer-term goal, not the same figure restated.
