Feature Landscape

Dronscape

A capability taxonomy for combat drones, organized by what a feature actually does — human/AI control balance, fleet coordination, hardware, comms, regulation — rather than by which company shipped it. Collapsed, this is just a feature list; expand any item for its detail, source, and timestamp.

Company-agnostic by design: the categories and spectrum tiers below are meant to hold features from any manufacturer. Right now every sourced feature happens to come from one program (Neros — see each item's tag), which is a gap in coverage, not a claim that Neros is the whole landscape. Two categories — Human/AI-Hybrid Control and Fleet & Swarm Capabilities — are framed as spectrums with placeholder tiers for capability levels no sourced example fills yet, so the shape of the taxonomy doesn't collapse down to whatever one source happened to mention.

Fleet & Swarm Capabilities

2 sourced · 2 conceptual

A spectrum from one operator flying one aircraft to fully decentralized multi-drone coordination. Most of today's fielded capability — in this source, anyway — sits at the low end of this ladder.

TIER 1One-to-Many Command & Control
A single ground station/operator manages multiple simultaneous radio links
Multi-Radio Management Interface Neros · Archer Live
Ground station UI supporting automated and manual mode switching across six distinct radios.
▶ 33:20
TIER 2Swarm-Scale Defense (Many-vs-Many)
Mass-produced interceptors fielded to match the volume of an incoming drone raid — the swarm is on the attacking side; defense is unit economics at scale
Kinetic Counter-UAS Defense Neros · Bandit Future
Drone-on-drone interceptor platform designed for base defense against loitering munitions such as Shahed-136 — defending against a swarm-scale raid requires fielding interceptors at comparable scale, which is what Bandit's shared component architecture (see Hardware) is built to enable.
▶ 16:15
TIER 3Coordinated Multi-Drone Tasking
Multiple drones share targets/sensor data and deconflict tasking among themselves, rather than each being independently piloted
Shared Situational Awareness & Task Deconfliction Concept
No sourced example here — this is the natural next rung above one-to-many C2: drones that know what their teammates are doing, not just what their own operator is telling them.
TIER 4Autonomous Swarm Behavior
Decentralized, self-organizing multi-drone coordination with no per-unit human controller
Decentralized Swarm Coordination Concept
No sourced example here — the top of the ladder as commonly discussed across the industry (emergent formation-keeping, distributed target allocation), not anything described in this source material.

Sourcing & methodology
Every sourced feature and timestamp above comes from a single source: "Manufacturing 1 million drones a year" — Olaf Hichwa, CTO of Neros (YouTube). Retrieved via one youtube:get_video_information tool call against that URL — no other web requests were made, so nothing here has been cross-checked against a second source, and no other company's products have been researched yet. "Live"/"Future" badges reflect how each feature was framed in the interview at the time of recording, not a live product-status feed. Items badged "Concept" have no sourced example at all — they exist only to keep the Human/AI-Hybrid Control and Fleet/Swarm spectrums from silently collapsing down to whatever one source happened to mention; they should be replaced or removed the moment a real example is found. The taxonomy (5 categories, spectrum tiers) is this page's own organizing structure, not something asserted by the source video.
