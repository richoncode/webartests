Making small apps & games pay — strategy notes

Judged one at a time, a small app/game is usually a lottery ticket (indie report: median ~$169/mo across 5,079 Stripe-verified Indie Hackers projects; ~5% of new apps reach $10K in two years per RevenueCat 2025). People who make it work don't rely on single apps:

  1. Portfolio of cheap bets: ship 10–20 tiny things a year, kill losers in weeks, double down on traction.
  2. Audience first: build logs/videos/posts make the app marketing; money comes from consulting, sponsors, courses, roles.
  3. Niches that pay: tools for people who earn money with them can charge $20–100/mo; consumer competes with free.
  4. Reuse one engine for many products: templates, asset packs, SDKs, B2B licensing (fits Quest/WebXR + spatial audio).
  5. Count indirect income: a playable demo can land consulting, talks, roles.

Takeaway: treat Perfect Fit and Chip Tycoon as public portfolio pieces and test balloons; ship fast, post on LinkedIn, double down only on whatever pulls people in.
