What Bolt is for, and what voice needs

NVIDIA pitches Bolt for voice agents, game characters and robots. It doesn't hear or speak on its own. It's the language model in the middle of a voice setup:

mic → speech-to-text → Bolt → text-to-speech → speaker

This page skips both speech ends. It's text in, text out.

For voice, the number that matters is how long after you stop talking until you hear the first word back. That includes speech-to-text, the model's first token, and text-to-speech. People usually want the whole thing under about 1–2 seconds. Tokens per second matters less, since speech is slow anyway.

A Mac running this in the browser can be fine for typed chat, but its first-token wait may be too slow for live voice. To close the gap: use a faster machine, turn thinking off or cap it, and keep the speech models local or on a fast cloud service.
