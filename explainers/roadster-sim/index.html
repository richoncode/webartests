How the launch is computed

  1. Rear tires carry 60% of weight plus load transfer (CG height ÷ wheelbase × a).
  2. Fan downforce adds normal load, but tires get less grippy per newton (load sensitivity).
  3. Thrusters push directly: F = g·Isp·ṁ, burning N₂ mass.

Isp derived from the video's hover numbers: Isp = F/(g·ṁ) = 20,000/(9.81×37) ≈ 55 s

Atmosphere does the work

Fans lower the pressure under the sealed skirt; 100 kPa of air on top pushes the car onto the surface. On a wall, friction (μ × suction) must beat weight. On a ceiling, suction alone must beat weight.

Full vacuum over the ~9 m² top would be 900 kN. Tesla runs 4×20 kW fans (80 kW) vs McMurtry's 2×30 kW. McMurtry filters 92% of debris by mass, so the finest 8% still erodes the fans, and skirts wear. Roughness here models a leaky seal.

Hover on cold gas

10 nozzles fed by 400 L + 200 L tanks at 10,000 PSI (~250 kg N₂), more than 80 kN peak. Every newton costs mass flow.

The video's stunt lasted ~4 s, inside the ~6.75 s budget. The video assumes a flat 20 kN (250 / 37 ≈ 6.75 s). With 250 kg of N₂ aboard, 20 kN can’t lift off, so auto-hover holds the true m·g. Try it and you get ~6 s once you count the climb.
