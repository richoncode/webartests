Spatial F1

OpenF1 engine data mapping

How a cached car_data window drives the Spatial F1 gauges and sound, what was left on the panel, and what a later Engine Sim hookup would change.

Unofficial fan demo. Not affiliated with Formula 1, the FIA, Formula One Management, or any team. OpenF1 is an independent API. Formula 1 and F1 are trademarks of their owners.

Why wire OpenF1 to an engine

The map page already ran a car around the Circuit of the Americas centerline at a made-up pace. OpenF1 publishes the live-timing stream teams and broadcasts use: throttle, RPM, gear, brake, speed, DRS, and a separate location channel. That is the honest input to an engine view. RPM and throttle say what the power unit is doing. Gear says when the driver shifted. Speed says how the car should travel. Location is a map problem, not an engine problem.

Engine Sim (Ange Yaghi) is the usual way to turn those inputs into a physical exhaust note. It integrates gas dynamics and plays the result. Stock Engine Sim is a desktop app. Its gear ratios are not Formula 1 ratios, and it does not ship an F1 turbo-hybrid script. This page records the mapping so a later sim can take the same fields without inventing a second interpretation of the trace.

The demo that uses this mapping is the Spatial F1 page.

Sample rate and interpolation

OpenF1 describes car_data at about 3.7 Hz. The cached window measures a little faster than that headline: 735 samples across 196.2 s, median spacing 0.240 s, shortest 0.159 s, longest 0.840 s.

  • RPM is linearly interpolated. Pitch glides across the gap instead of stair-stepping every quarter second.
  • Speed is linearly interpolated, then integrated with the trapezoid rule into metres (s on each sample). That distance moves the car.
  • Throttle, brake, DRS, and gear use the sample at or before the clock. A throttle drop from 80 or more to 20 or less (15 of those in this window) arrives as a step. The synth follows a cut in a few tens of milliseconds. It does not ramp the pedal across the sample gap.
  • Gear is an event. This window has 86 gear changes, gears 2 through 8. Each change dips the synth gain for about a tenth of a second. The RPM trace already contains the shift, so the dip is only an accent.

The speed control (0.5×, 1×, 3×, 10×) scales the same clock the scrubber sets. Pause holds the clock. Sound, gauges, and distance stay on that clock.

Speed check

OpenF1 car_data.speed is km/h. The cache stores it as spd. Nothing in the player caps it. The gauge is Math.round of the interpolated sample. Motion uses metres integrated from that km/h with the trapezoid rule (s on each sample, speed ÷ 3.6).

0.000222 km/hMatches the live API sample at 2025-10-19T20:29:28.163Z (rpm 11542, gear 5, throttle 100). The next five speeds, 227, 234, 239, 243, 249, match too.
0.000 → 0.320222 then 227Δs is 19.956 m. That is exactly the trapezoid of 222 and 227 km/h over 0.320 s (224.5 km/h average).

Across all 735 samples the stored s matches a fresh trapezoid of spd to within a millimetre. The player then multiplies distance by 2 × centerline / 10872.986, about 1.009, so the two laps close on the smoothed line. That stretch is geometric. It is not a speed limit, and the km/h on the panel stays the telemetry value.

Dyno lock and a free engine

Dyno lock holds RPM at the telemetry value and takes throttle from the trace. Load and tone follow the recording. A free engine would take throttle and gear as driver inputs and integrate RPM from torque, drag, and the gearbox. That RPM leaves the recorded trace as soon as the ratios, inertia, or aero drag disagree with the real car. Stock Engine Sim ratios would do that immediately. The demo ships dyno lock only.

What you hear is a browser synth whose pitch still follows RPM. The default voicing is a 4-stroke V6: three combustion events per revolution, so the firing frequency is rpm / 60 × 3. At 11,500 rpm that is about 575 Hz. The partials are the saw and square stack from the Spatial F1 engine before the sine preset (the fundamental is a saw, not a sine). They are colored by the live FireSim Audio resonators: bandpasses at 280 Hz (Q 0.8), 1500 Hz (Q 3.2), and 3400 Hz (Q 4.5), with gains 0.55 / 0.9 / 0.42, a brown-noise pole of 0.97, and a tanh drive of 1.35. Those numbers are the PhISEM defaults in experiments/firesimaudio. The direct low-pass stays under about 1.6 kHz so the old open-filter buzz does not come back. This repo has no separate Engine Sim page to copy. Lifted throttle at high RPM stays audible (overrun) and quieter. This is not exhaust-gas synthesis.

Engine presets

The Sound button still starts the audio context. The Engine menu swaps voicing without touching the trace. Each preset is a JSON file in engines/. RPM interpolation, the throttle step, and the gear-change dip stay as described above. The file changes the firing multiple, an optional pitch scale, the oscillators, how far the low-pass opens, and how loud a closed throttle is.

FilePresetWhat changes
engines/v6-highrev.jsonHigh-rev V6Default. Three pulses per revolution. Saw on the fundamental and at half speed, a square octave up, copied from the pre-sine engine. Parallel FireSim Audio formants at 280 / 1500 / 3400 Hz. Direct low-pass mix 0.4, opening to about 1.6 kHz. tanh drive 1.35.
engines/v8-throaty.jsonThroaty V8Four pulses per revolution, then pitchScale 0.5 so the same RPM sits lower than the V6. Stronger low harmonics, more saturation, and a slow lope (capped near 11 Hz so it stays a pulse, not a second buzz).
engines/idle-heavy.jsonIdle-heavyV6 firing multiple, lower pitch scale, a filter that barely opens, and a high gain floor with the pedal up. A lifted throttle stays a lumpy rumble.
engines/muted.jsonMutedRPM tracking kept, level dropped to a quiet sine and a shut filter. The audio button shows whether that output is on, and turns it off completely when audio is off.

Presets do not change dyno lock. A V8 voicing does not mean the cached car was a V8. It is a different reading of the same RPM and throttle.

An omitted filter.mix means the direct low-pass is fully open (mix 1). Only the V6 file sets mix (0.4), because the formants carry the rest of that voice. The loader treats a missing mix as 1. It must not turn the missing number into NaN: assigning NaN to an AudioParam throws while the graph is built, and the oscillators never start. The speaker button still resumes the AudioContext inside the click and starts the oscillators only after the context is running. That is true for every preset, not only V6.

Surroundings

Probes leave the car a few times a second. Side rays sit at 2°, 5°, and 7° and repeat every 10° from about 32° off the nose through abeam to about 152° behind, on the left and the right. The 7° band is aimed at about 80% of the height of the grandstands beside the car. A shorter building clears that band, so it lights fewer rays. Those side rays are about four fifths of the probes, so a dual-sided grandstand lights most of them and the wet sum reaches Max reverb. Open air sums at Min reverb. A short building stays near that floor. The wet sum is curved, so the gap from open air up to a both-sides stadium is much wider than a straight mix. Min reverb and Max reverb set that floor and that ceiling. Defaults are 0.05 and 1.15. Max runs from 0 to 30, so a fan that is only partly lit can still be turned up. Those two sliders are the wet bus. A second set is an arc overhead: 16° and 34° ahead of the nose (a 16° ray meets a 6.5 m deck about 20 m out), steeper rays straight up, and the same shallow pair behind the tail. They read Mapbox Streets buildings, grass, scrub, and wood, footbridge centerlines thickened into a short deck, and tree models when the tile has them. Tiles are fetched for the whole circuit, so a grandstand beside the car still counts when the chase view is looking down the straight. A venue-sized park polygon is not vegetation: the circuit sits inside one, and counting it would hide open asphalt. A bridge hit on the overhead arc is weighted about 2.8×. Under the deck the whole fan hits that short reflection, the wet sum reaches the same cap as a both-sides grandstand, and the delay bar follows it. Those rays rise on the way in and the way out. A grandstand on those rays is not boosted. Hits become an I3DL2-style send on the dry engine. The dry path is not filtered. The send is mixed after the compressor, so the note does not duck the tail. With every probe a miss, that wet sum is Min reverb (0.05 by default), much drier than a both-sides grandstand at Max reverb (1.15 by default). Min reverb and Max reverb move the open-air floor and the both-sides / bridge ceiling without changing that curve. Max runs to 30. Min and Max are the wet bus, so the speakers follow the meter. Reverb off closes it. Crowd, wind, and tire are a separate, simpler layer: they do not change that room.

Under the reverb controls, nine bars stay in a fixed stack. Each label sits in a reserved column to the left of its bar, and the number sits to the right, so the words do not cover the fill and a longer reading does not move the row. Wet mix is the probe sum between Min and Max. The bar fills at Max. Delay is the loudest probe (full scale 450 ms). Nearness of that surface fills as it gets closer; the number is still metres. Tail feedback uses full scale 0.34. Crowd distance fills as the nearest grandstand point gets closer, over 100 m. Crowd volume, wind volume, and tire volume use the same 0–1.5 scale as their sliders. Tire pitch runs from about 740 Hz quiet to about 2940 Hz at the loudest screech. The line above the bars names the surface and does not grow the stack. A top-down map stays on the right of those levers, ahead up, the car a triangle, misses faint, hits in the material color, and a ring when a hit is almost overhead. The levers sit on two rows so that map has room: Reverb, Min, and Max on the first, Crowd, Wind, and Tire on the second. Open asphalt reads “Open air”. Reverb off zeros the four room bars and leaves the crowd, wind, and tire meters running, and leaves the dry engine. Show draws the same probes. A hit takes the material color — glass, concrete, or grass — and thickens up to eight times the 5 cm hairline when the surface is a couple of metres away. A miss stays a faint hairline.

Crowd uses the same preloaded Streets tiles. Each building whose type or class is grandstand or stadium is one section (a tile boundary can split a stand into two). Points are laid along that footprint’s long axis, at least two per section, about every 34 m, and capped near 56 so a lap stays light. Each point loops one shared bed through its own panner: equal-power pan, and a playback-rate Doppler from the car’s speed toward that point. Level is full within about 50 m of that point, falls off to silence at 100 m, and stays silent farther down the track, so a grandstand you have already passed does not keep murmuring. The bed is speech-band murmur with cheer swells, and a few sparse cues so it reads as people: a rising whistle, a short plastic horn, a vowel shout, and a clap. Per-source gain is high enough that Crowd at 1.5× is obvious with the engine muted, and 0 is silent. A compressor on the crowd bus keeps a close grandstand from clipping. Web Audio does not Doppler a panner on its own. Wind is a second noise loop, high-passed and band-passed. Its level and a mild brightness follow OpenF1 km/h, quiet near the slowest samples and full near 300 km/h. Tire is a third loop, high-passed into a narrow bandpass. Its level and centre frequency follow cornering g and brake g: each axis levels off so a hard corner and a hard brake together are louder and higher than either one, and a straight with the brake flag up is silent. A fast sample on a tight centerline bend can read as tens of g because the racing line is not fitted; that still caps at the same hard-corner level. Crowd, Wind, and Tire scale those buses from 0 to 1.5×. The speaker button silences crowd, wind, and tire with the engine. Engine off leaves those three running.

What this demo ships

  1. Cache. openf1-9888-driver1.json — driver 1 (VER), Austin race session 9888, laps 52 and 53. Fetched once from car_data with date>=2025-10-19T20:29:28.112 and date<2025-10-19T20:32:44.386. The page does not call the API.
  2. Why this window. Two flying laps that loop cleanly: both ends are about 222 km/h, gear 5, full throttle, RPM near 11,500. Lap 53 is this car's fastest lap of that race, 98.107 s. Across the window RPM runs 5,454–12,032, throttle 0–100, speed 74–308 km/h, brake on for 128 samples.
  3. Panel. Gear, RPM, speed, lap plus distance through that lap, throttle, brake, DRS, and a scrubber, tied to Play and the speed control. Step back and Step forward, beside Pause, move one cached sample and hold the clock. Engine, crowd, wind, tire, and reverb keep sounding that moment. Reverb starts the second control row, with Min reverb, Max reverb, and Show, then Crowd, Wind, and Tire, and a fixed stack of nine bars: wet, delay, near, tail, crowd distance, crowd volume, wind volume, tire volume, and tire pitch. Readouts keep a reserved width so a new material or a longer number does not move the row. Engine on, beside the speaker, mutes only the engine.
  4. Sound. The Web Audio dyno above, with the four presets in engines/. The audio button creates or resumes the AudioContext inside that click, plays a one-sample buffer while the gesture is still active, and only then starts the oscillators, once the context is running. Every preset takes that path. A missing filter.mix is 1, not an empty number, so Throaty V8, idle-heavy, and muted build a graph instead of throwing. If resume stays suspended, the icon stays a muted speaker and the status line says sound failed to start. A later click tries again. While the note is playing the icon is a speaker with waves. The default preset is high-rev V6. The probe set adds the surroundings send described above. Reverb is on until the toggle closes the wet bus. Open air is a drier send, not silence. Reverb off leaves the dry engine. With audio on, grandstand points, the speed-dependent wind, and the tire screech play on their own output. Muting the audio button silences those too. Engine off ducks the engine and its reverb send and leaves crowd, wind, and tire running, so those can be checked on their own. Turning the speaker back on keeps that engine mute.
  5. Car. Still the centerline loop, on the smoothed line the ribbon uses. Integrated speed is 10,873 m. Two loops of that smoothed centerline are 10,969 m (5,484.5 m × 2). The player multiplies distance by 1.009 so the window closes on the loop. The racing line is not fitted, so braking is not aligned to corners.
  6. Camera. Chase is the default. The rest pose is 31.5 m behind the model origin and pitch 85°, Mapbox's most level look, so the aim is the horizon rather than the roof. A critically damped spring (about 5 rad/s) follows that pose. The spring's position is the lens, held behind the car on its heading. Mapbox's look point is derived from that lens, so a corner eases the camera instead of shoving it off the rear. The target's velocity is fed forward, so a straight holds the distance and a corner eases instead of cutting. Choosing Chase from Overview seeds the spring at the current camera and flies it into that pose over about a second and a half. The car eases from 16 m down to 5.5 m across the same flight. Overview fits the circuit (pitch 55°, bearing −20°) and draws the car at 16 m. Heading is the direction through the centerline a few metres either side of the car, in local east/north, and that angle yaws the model. OpenF1 has no yaw to copy: car_data has no position, and location has x, y, and z only. If terrain between the lens and the car would hide it, pitch eases down from 85° just enough to clear the crest, beginning a short way before the hill, and eases back when the sight line is open. The lens stays behind the car. Drag, rotate, or pitch the chase view to look around; on release the offset eases back over about half a second. Scroll zoom stays off in chase. ?head= adds radians to the model. The default is 0: the glTF nose is +X, which already lies along the direction of travel. Passing π turns the car around.
  7. Map. Mapbox Standard (mapbox://styles/mapbox/standard), dusk light, 3D buildings and 3D trees on, pedestrian roads on. Road names, route shields, path labels, and place labels are hidden. The centerline is resampled every 2 m, blurred with an 8 m gaussian, and sampled every 2.5 m. Those GeoJSON sources set simplification tolerance to 0 so the map does not turn the curve back into kinks. The circuit is a 15 m asphalt fill with a concrete shoulder (11 m from that line) under the yellow stroke and its black casing. Street and minor road strokes are widened to real-world metres (about 9 m and 7.5 m) with a light sidewalk casing, and the style road color is asphalt gray so polygon roads match. Terrain stays on the style DEM. Exaggeration is about 1.35 around the whole-circuit zoom and eases to 1 at the chase zoom, and the car is lifted to that exaggerated ground so it stays on the ribbon. The projection is Mercator.

Later

None of this is in the demo. The mapping above is what those pieces should consume.

  • Custom V6 script. A Formula 1 power-unit description in Engine Sim's .mr format: 1.6 L V6, the rev range in this trace, and ratios that can sit beside dyno lock. Stock example engines are the wrong machine.
  • Turbo and wastegate sound. Stock Engine Sim builds the note from exhaust pulses. It has no separate blow-off or wastegate sample. Community turbo pieces change airflow and power; they do not add that hiss. A fork would add the valve as its own event, keyed off throttle lifts while boost is up.
  • WASM, live sim. People have compiled Engine Sim v0.1.11a with Emscripten and hosted the desktop app in a page (the trail starts at engine-sim#428). Those builds are the whole program: starter, ignition, keyboard, on-screen dyno. They do not offer a supported way to pin RPM and throttle to this scrubber, and they are large next to a map page. When a build can be driven in dyno lock from the same clock, it can replace the synth without changing the JSON.
  • Forced-induction fork. Same codebase, with the blow-off path and a hybrid deployment model if the trace grows an MGU field. OpenF1 car_data does not carry boost or energy-store state today.
  • Multi-car line fit. The page can show NOR, LEC, and PIA (openf1-9888-driver4.json, driver16, driver81) on the same centerline. The toggle is off until you turn it on. Place is s_lap / reference_m along that line: metres integrated from each driver’s own lap start, not a copy of driver 1’s distance. At the window start those fractions are 0.924, 0.920, and 0.739. Projecting the same instant of OpenF1 location onto driver 1’s lap gives 0.925, 0.919, and 0.737. The racing line is still not fitted, so a car in the pit lane would still be drawn on the centerline.
  • Session 11396. A later Austin race. Cache it the same way once OpenF1 has the timing. This page does not fetch it.
  • Location fit. OpenF1 location is the timing x/y grid. Mapping that grid onto the centerline (or replacing the centerline) would put braking on the right corners and let more than one car share the map.
  • Free engine mode. Optional, beside dyno lock, once ratios and drag are close enough that integrated RPM is worth comparing to the trace. The trace stays the reference.
