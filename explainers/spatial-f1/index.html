Spatial F1 demo

A low-poly F1 car on the Circuit of the Americas centerline, about real length in the chase view. The camera is a damped spring a fixed distance behind the car and looks level toward the horizon. The circuit is a smoothed 15 m asphalt ribbon with a concrete shoulder, and Mapbox Standard streets use that same pavement color, with sidewalks along local roads. 3D buildings and trees stay on, the light is dusk, and the ground keeps the terrain’s rise and fall. RPM, throttle, and gear follow a cached OpenF1 trace and drive the sound. Unofficial; not affiliated with Formula 1.

What this is

The yellow line is the COTA centerline (about 5.5 km). The line is resampled every couple of metres and blurred with a short gaussian before the asphalt ribbon and the yellow stroke are built, and the map is told not to simplify those shapes, so a coarse vertex does not leave a pointed corner. A black casing keeps it readable. The car rides that loop on a 15 m asphalt ribbon with a concrete shoulder generated from the same smoothed line. Mapbox Standard still supplies the surrounding streets, 3D buildings, and 3D trees, lit at dusk. The ground uses Mapbox terrain so the hills stay visible when the chase view pitches down; exaggeration eases toward real scale at the chase zoom. Road names, route shields, and place labels are off, so the course does not read as a navigation map. Local streets and minor roads are redrawn at real-world width in asphalt gray, with a light sidewalk on the curb. Time on the scrubber is a cached OpenF1 car_data window, and speed from that window moves the car. Distance is scaled by about 0.9% so the two laps meet this centerline. The OpenF1 x/y grid is not fitted onto the map, so a brake in the trace is not pinned to the matching corner. The cache itself has no coordinates and no yaw: car_data is speed, RPM, gear, throttle, brake, and DRS. OpenF1 location, which this page does not load, is only x, y, and z in a track grid, also with no heading. The nose is the centerline’s direction of travel. Other cars uses the same line for driver 4 (NOR), driver 16 (LEC), and driver 81 (PIA). Each marker sits where that driver’s own OpenF1 speed, integrated from the start of their lap, meets the start/finish. The wedges are drawn large enough to read on the whole circuit, in an unlit team color, with the driver’s acronym. In this window those three run behind driver 1, so the chase camera, which looks forward, also keeps each acronym on screen. The detailed car stays driver 1.

Gauges and the synth read the same clock. RPM is interpolated. Throttle and gear stay as steps, and a gear change dips the sound. Brake, speed, and DRS are shown on the panel and do not retune the synth. The Engine menu loads a voicing from engines/. High-rev V6 is the default: the saw and square firing stack from the earlier Spatial F1 engine, colored with the live FireSim Audio resonator centers. Throaty V8, idle-heavy, and muted stay optional. The synth is Web Audio, a stand-in until a WASM Engine Sim can take the same inputs. Rays on the sides sit only a few degrees up and a little forward of straight out, so they meet a grandstand or building face instead of clearing the roof. Rays ahead of the nose stay low for the same reason. A separate arc looks up: shallow rays ahead of the nose, steeper rays overhead, and the same shallow rays behind the tail, a few degrees left and right. A bridge on that arc weighs more, so a footbridge spikes the wet bar and shortens the delay on the way in, under the deck, and on the way out. A grandstand on those rays is not boosted. They read buildings, footbridges, grass, bushes, and trees from the circuit’s map tiles, not only from what the chase view happens to have loaded. A venue-sized park polygon is not vegetation. Glass, concrete, and grass change a short reverb on the engine: delay and wetness from the distance, brightness from the material. The dry note is not filtered. Reverb on mixes that send after the compressor. The bars under those controls show the loudest probe: wet mix, delay, nearness, and tail feedback, in a fixed slot so the numbers do not move the row. Send multiplies the wet path from 0 to 3×. Engine on leaves crowd, wind, and tire running and mutes only the engine, including its reverb send. Open air stays drier than a wall and still has a send. Reverb off is the dry engine only. Show colors a hit by material and thickens it, up to eight times the hairline, when the surface is close. A miss stays a faint hairline. Crowd is a short loop of layered murmurs and cheer bursts on several points along each Mapbox grandstand or stadium footprint, panned from the car, so a pass-by changes level and Doppler. The Crowd slider runs from silent to a strong stadium level. A whistle, a plastic horn, a short shout, and a clap sit on that bed so the stands read as people rather than filtered noise. Wind is filtered noise, louder and a little brighter as the OpenF1 speed rises. Tire screech is a separate noise loop, high-passed and run through a narrow bandpass on that same output. Cornering g is the car’s speed squared times the curvature of this smoothed centerline, over an 18 m chord, divided by gravity. It is the line the car rides, not a fitted racing line, so a fast sample on a tight bend reads as a hard corner and then stops getting louder. Brake g is how fast the OpenF1 speed falls while the brake flag is down. This cache is only 0 or 100, and a full flag floors the read so a blunt window still speaks. Flag up, including a lift on a straight, is silent. Hard corner and hard brake together are louder and higher than either one. The audio button mutes crowd, wind, and tire.

Chase is the default camera. Its rest pose sits about 31.5 m behind the car anchor, and Mapbox pitch settles at 85°, the most level look the map allows, so the aim is the horizon. A critically damped spring follows that pose. The spring tracks the lens itself, held on the car's heading behind the rear, so a corner eases the camera instead of swinging it out beside the car. On a straight the distance stays put, because the spring is fed the target's velocity, and a corner eases instead of snapping. Choosing Chase from Overview flies the same spring from the current view into that pose, and the car eases from the overview size down to 5.5 m. Overview fits the whole circuit and draws the car at 16 m so it stays findable. Heading is the direction through the few metres of centerline either side of the car, in local east and north, and that angle yaws the car. When the ground between the lens and the car rises through the line of sight, pitch eases down from 85° just enough to lift the lens clear of the crest, beginning a short way before the hill, then eases back. The lens stays behind the car. Drag to slide the chase view. Right-drag, or ctrl-drag, turns and pitches it. The camera holds still while the pointer is down, and on release the offset eases back. ?zoom=, ?pitch=, and ?back= (metres behind the anchor) tune the chase rest pose. ?head= adds radians to the model. The default is 0, which points the nose along the direction of travel.
