Wed 9 Sep 2026 · 19:45 PDT There is no partial refresh to findClosed Three ways to refresh less than the whole panel, all dead ends: the partial macro is undefined for this driver, the window command is empty, and the waveform is 99.6% of the cost.

What the class offers

EPaper subclasses TFT_eSprite, so all drawing lands in an in-RAM framebuffer and only a handful of calls reach the panel:

CallWhat it does
fillScreen() / fillSprite()writes the RAM buffer; no panel activity at all
update()pushes the whole buffer and runs the waveform
update(x,y,w,h,data)pushes a region from a caller-supplied buffer
updataPartial(x,y,w,h)the library's partial-refresh path (spelling is theirs)
sleep() / wake()panel power state

Clearing is therefore always two steps — fill the buffer, then update(). Nothing skips the waveform.

Three reasons a region refresh cannot work here

  • The partial path does not exist for this driver. Combo 521 selects ED2208_DRIVER, and updataPartial() ends by calling EPD_UPDATE_PARTIAL(). That macro appears zero times in ED2208_Defines.h, with no #ifndef fallback anywhere in the library — the call would not compile. Six drivers do define it, all mono or black/white/red/yellow. None is Spectra 6.
  • There is no window to set. EPD_SET_WINDOW on ED2208 is do { } while (0). Even the full-frame path is not windowing anything; the macro survives for drivers that support it.
  • The data would land in the wrong place. EPD_PUSH_NEW_COLORS issues command 0x10 and streams w/2 × h bytes into panel RAM from offset zero. Without a window a 200×100 region would overwrite the top-left corner rather than appear at (x,y). The x and y arguments only reach pushImage into the local sprite buffer; they never leave the microcontroller.

And there would be nothing to win

The refresh cost is not data transfer. EPD_UPDATE() is a bare 0x12 with no region parameter, so it drives the whole panel through the Spectra colour passes however many bytes were sent:

frame buffer              192,000 bytes at 4 bpp
update() pushes OLD+NEW   384,000 bytes

measured refresh           37.9 s
  transfer at 20 MHz        0.15 s    0.4%
  waveform                 37.7 s   99.6%

Pushing a single 8×8 block instead of the entire frame would recover 0.15 s of 37.9 s. Attempting it would have corrupted the panel, taken the same 38 seconds, and needed a second full refresh to recover, so it was not flashed.

Settled: the flicker is a fixed cost per repaint, confirmed from the driver source rather than taken from the specification sheet. The only lever is how often the panel repaints, which the content hash already minimizes.

Correction

Two earlier notes were wrong and are corrected here. The controller is driven as ED2208, not the UC8179C that Seeed's wiki names — UC8179_Defines.h is a separate driver for a different board, and it is one of the six that does implement partial refresh, which is probably how the confusion arose. And update(x,y,w,h,data) was described as possibly shortening the refresh; the waveform is 99.6% of the cost and panel-wide by command design, so it cannot.

Wed 9 Sep 2026 · 19:10 PDT Cake and eagle icons, and two collisions found by measuringFlashed The star was a pentagon. PRIME sat on the mission name. Two further overlaps only showed up when the column widths were worked out on paper rather than looked at.

The test frames earned their keep

Pressing white-right put both band cases on the panel within a minute, which is what the mode was for. Test mode 2 confirmed the outlined SMOKE chip reads at a glance and that a red battery bar and PLUG IN NOW land correctly.

The star was a pentagon

drawStar filled triangles from the centre to each pair of adjacent vertices, which tiles a solid pentagon rather than a star. The yellow blob on both frames was that. Replaced outright, since birthdays and Wildlife Day now have icons of their own.

Event kindIcon
EVT_BIRTHDAYlayer cake — paper body, yellow frosting, three candles with lit flames
EVT_WILDLIFEsoaring eagle — swept wings, body, yellow beak, paper silhouette outlined in ink

At 34 px a silhouette reads as a bird; any attempt at feather detail turns to mud on this panel. CalendarEvent gained a kind field, so the icon follows from the date rather than from parsing the label.

PRIME needed a column, not a gap

The pill was placed at the measured end of the mission name plus ten pixels, which put it over the last characters. The band is now three columns with reserved widths — name, a 66 px PRIME slot at x = 544, and a 168 px time column — so the pill cannot land on text whatever the name length. It also gained an ink outline, matching the air chip.

Two collisions that only arithmetic found

Working the widths out rather than eyeballing them turned up two more faults, both of which would have shipped:

  • PM - 45 min after sunset is 24 characters. Right-aligned in the time column it reached back to x = 586, inside PRIME's slot at 602. AM/PM now travels with the time — 8:14 PM — leaving 45 min after sunset at 152 px, comfortably clear.
  • The longest real mission name, FALCON 9 - TRANSPORTER 18 (DEDICATED SSO RIDESHARE), overflows the name column at 18 pt and at 12 pt. There were only two steps, so it would have run into PRIME. A third step at 9 pt fits it in 408 px.
  • launchTime[8] could not hold 11:52 PM — nine bytes with the terminator.

PLUG IN NOW was a fixed 178 px pill the words very nearly filled; it is now sized from the measured text with even padding and vertically centred.

The mockup was broken, and silently

Cause: earlier mockup edits used bare string replacement with no assertion, so a pattern that failed to match printed a success message and changed nothing. layout() had grown a third parameter while its only call site still passed two, and the footer referenced a runway variable that had been split into runwayN and runwayL. The file parsed; it just could not run.

Every edit now asserts its match, and mockup-render-check.js executes the page's own script against a minimal DOM across the full cross-product of controls — day, sky, launch, calendar, air, battery, runway — checking that the panel and the geometry table both produce output. It reports 3240 combinations rendering cleanly and would have failed on both of the above immediately.

Deep sleep had been waking on ext0, which watches exactly one pin.   4. a wet week at 90% precipitation, moderate air, plus bins

The header reads TEST MODE n - not live data in place of the timestamp, because a synthetic frame that looks real is worse than no test mode at all. Returning to live clears the stored hash so the next live wake definitely repaints.

The mockup caught up

The mockup had fallen several changes behind the firmware — no air chip, no footer reflow, no DAYLIGHT, no event band — which makes it a poor specification. It now carries the event band, the shared row, a Calendar control listing all five dates, the outlined air chip and the two- and three-line footer columns.

Wed 9 Sep 2026 · 18:05 PDT Contrast, footer overlap, and change detection provedVerified Yellow type on paper was near-invisible, the runway line was overlapping the credits, and the repaint test was impossible because three hashed fields had stopped being drawn.

Yellow on paper does not read

Green and red type hold up on this panel; yellow does not, and an air-quality reading is exactly the field that must be legible from across a room. The number is now always ink, and the band name sits on a filled chip with a black border — ink on yellow, paper on green and red — so GOOD, MODERATE and SMOKE all read at one strength. Every segment of the band bar is outlined whether lit or not, so the yellow one has an edge instead of bleeding into the paper.

The footer was overlapping, not merely wide

94 days until recharge at 18 pt reached x = 658 while the source credits began at x = 553. The two were drawn over each other; the photograph shows the collision.

ColumnWasNow
Runway262 – 658, one 18 pt line262 – 363, 92 days at 12 pt over until recharge at 9 pt
Credits553 – 778, two lines692 – 778, three lines
Gap−105 px, overlapping329 px

Why the repaint test could not be run

The complaint was that change detection ignores the update-time text so the behavior can be tested. asOf was in fact never hashed. The real fault was three fields that were still hashed but had stopped being drawn: nowTemp, nowCode and condition all left the panel when the current temperature and its condition line came out of the header. The screen was repainting because the outdoor temperature had moved a degree, with nothing on the panel to show it.

Rule: the hash covers exactly what is drawn, and nothing else. A field that is hashed but not drawn repaints the screen for a change nobody can see. A field that is drawn but not hashed leaves a stale value on the panel.

That second half applied too: the battery had been hashed in 5% buckets to suppress repaints, which allowed the panel to display a percentage that no longer matched the reading behind it. Battery and runway are now hashed exactly. At about 1 %/day that costs roughly one repaint a day, which is the price of the number on screen being true.

Proved across two boots

run 1   draw: repainting, hash 11bc5646 -> c64d8bbb
        draw: full refresh took 37937 ms
        total cycle ~48 s

run 2   draw: skipped, content unchanged (hash c64d8bbb)
        panel never touched
        total cycle ~10 s

The same hash across two separate power-on boots means the NVS copy survives a power cycle, not just the RTC copy across deep sleep. A wake that finds nothing new now costs ten seconds of radio and no flicker at all.

Refresh time

Measured 37.9 s this round against 35.8 s earlier, both well past the 15–20 s Seeed publishes. Nothing in the sketch influences it; the number is what it is, which is why not repainting matters as much as it does.

Next

  • The 06:00 wake is the first timer wake rather than a power-on, and the first to read the hash from RTC_DATA_ATTR rather than fall back to NVS.
  • Read the air chip on the panel — whether ink on yellow is genuinely legible at distance is a judgement that cannot be made from a screenshot.

Wed 9 Sep 2026 · 17:20 PDT Battery estimate, wake cadence, and the last failing fetchAll three parse A 607,333-day runway decoded to a 1970 baseline. Three days of unattended running supplied the real drain rate, and the launch fetch finally parses.

Three days unattended

The board ran on battery from the 6th to the 9th with no cable attached, waking and repainting on its own. Two frames a day apart gave the first real drain measurement:

WhenBatteryRunway shown
Tue 8 Sep, 05:5788%607,333 days
Wed 9 Sep, 05:5787%450,346 days

1.0 %/day, which is what Seeed's three-month figure implies. The hardware is behaving; only the arithmetic was wrong.

The 1970 baseline

607,333 days from 88% implies a drain of 0.000145 %/day, which implies an elapsed baseline of about 20,690 days — 56.6 years. That is the distance from the epoch. estimateRunwayDays() was being called before syncClock(), so the very first boot anchored its baseline to a 1970 timestamp and every later reading divided a small drop by half a century.

Two defenses. The estimate now runs only after NTP has set the clock, and any stored timestamp earlier than November 2023 is discarded and re-anchored rather than trusted.

Measured prior instead of "learning"

The old version refused to answer until it had four samples and a 3% drop, which is why the panel said "learning drain rate" for three days. The 1.0 %/day measured above is now the starting estimate, so the first frame after a flash shows a real number. Measured history displaces it over about a week, weighted by how long the window is — a single day of readings at 1% resolution is far too noisy to stand on. The rate is clamped to 0.2–20 %/day, so the worst a corrupt baseline can now print is 500 days rather than 607,333.

scenario                             days   %/day
first frame, no history    87%         87    1.00
1 %/day over 1 day         87%         87    1.00
7% over 7 days             81%         81    1.00
the old 1970 case          88%        440    0.20  (clamped)
5% drop in one day         85%         54    1.57
40% over 4 days            50%          8    6.14

Two wakes a day, at the right two

Twelve-hour intervals, and the flicker of a full repaint makes that the right trade. Aligning a 12 h interval to midnight would have woken it at 00:00 and 12:00 — no use to a morning dashboard. The alignment is anchored to WAKE_HOUR_MORNING, so it wakes at 06:00 and 18:00; every hour from 00 to 23 was checked to confirm it schedules one of those two.

The panel had also been advertising next 06:00 while the timer was set for twelve hours later, because the displayed time and the sleep duration were computed separately. One function now feeds both.

The launch fetch, finally

Every run since first light had failed here with IncompleteInput, and forcing HTTP/1.0 had not helped. Cloudflare answers Connection: close with no Content-Length, so a streaming parse ends the first time a pause between TLS records leaves available() at zero. The 4 kB weather and 354-byte air responses arrive in a single burst and survived it; the 26 kB launch list never did. Reading the body to completion before parsing fixes it:

api.open-meteo.com             parsed  4,496 bytes
air-quality-api.open-meteo.com parsed    354 bytes
ll.thespacedevs.com            parsed 26,352 bytes
runway: 93 days until recharge
draw: full refresh took 35768 ms
sleeping 2519 s (0.7 h)          -> 18:00

Display

The footer reads 93 days until recharge — no tilde, no caption above it. A tilde in front of a number that is already an estimate only added a character to decode.

Still standing

  • Every run so far has been a power-on wake. A timer wake is what exercises the RTC hash path, and the aligned sleep now makes that observable at 06:00 or 18:00.
  • The full refresh measures 35.8 s against Seeed's quoted 15–20 s, consistently.
  • The serial device number changes between plug-ins — it was usbserial-210, now usbserial-110. build.sh discovers it rather than hard-coding it.

Sun 6 Sep 2026 · 17:30 PDT First light — running on the panelLive Five bugs between a verified flash and a working dashboard: a boot loop from the app offset, blank text from a digits-only font, and every fetch defeated by chunked transfer encoding.

Boot loop — the app went to the wrong address

The first flash verified every hash and then boot-looped on invalid magic byte. arduino-cli honours a custom partitions.csv for the table it writes but not for where it puts the app: it always writes the application at 0x10000 and boot_app0 at 0xe000. This board's factory layout reserves 500 kB of NVS, which pushes otadata to 0x86000 and app0 to 0x90000, so the bootloader looked where the table said and found nothing.

0x0      bootloader
0x8000   partitions
0x86000  boot_app0    (otadata, not the usual 0xe000)
0x90000  app          (app0,    not the usual 0x10000)

build.sh --upload now flashes with esptool at those offsets instead of calling arduino-cli upload.

Upload speed

arduino-cli defaults to 921600 baud and the upload died immediately after the rate change — the same CH340 ceiling found while reading the factory image back. The FQBN now pins UploadSpeed=115200.

Blank text — font 6 has no letters

The weekday, date and every large label were blank. Built-in fonts 6, 7 and 8 contain only 1234567890:-.apm — a digits-and-clock set — so any letter drawn in font 6 renders as nothing. Only the numerals had survived. Font 4 tops out at 26 px, so the whole layout moved to the Adafruit free fonts that LOAD_GFXFF already compiles in: FreeSansBold at 9, 12, 18 and 24 pt. Those are bold at every size, which also answered the small labels being hard to read.

Including the font headers explicitly duplicates every glyph table — TFT_eSPI has already compiled them in.

Every fetch failed — chunked transfer encoding

Wi-Fi joined and NTP synced, yet all three endpoints returned HTTP 200 and then InvalidInput. getSize() reported -1, which was the clue: both APIs answer HTTP/1.1 with chunked encoding, and HTTPClient::getStream() hands back the raw socket with the chunk-size framing still in it. ArduinoJson was reading 1a3b\r\n{. HTTPClient only removes that framing inside getString().

http.useHTTP10(true) settles it — HTTP/1.0 has no chunked encoding, so the body parses as a stream and the filter keeps the document small. Weather and air quality parsed on the next flash.

The launch request was too large to read

Asking for five launches in detailed mode is ~71 kB and the TLS read did not survive it: IncompleteInput. It now runs in two steps — the ~25 kB list carries net, name and id, enough to decide whether anything qualifies tonight, and one launch is fetched in detail only when one does. A failure in the second step costs the landing note, not the band.

Repaint only when the picture changes

A full refresh measured 35.8 s, well past Seeed's quoted 15–20 s, and drives the panel through twenty or thirty visible color passes. The Model now hashes exactly the fields that reach the screen, kept in RTC_DATA_ATTR across deep sleep and in NVS across a power cycle. An unchanged hash skips the draw and the refresh entirely.

The hash deliberately excludes the fetch time and rounds the battery to the nearest 5%, or every wake would differ and the whole exercise would be pointless. A failed fetch draws nothing at all and leaves the last good frame up.

Sun 6 Sep 2026 · 16:35 PDT Live endpoints checked against the parser3 bugs Booster landing data was absent from the response the firmware requested, the sky test read the wrong hour, and a 50-character mission name truncated mid-word. All three found before flashing.

Method

The three URLs were rebuilt from config.h itself rather than retyped, fetched live, and every JSON path fetch.h reads was checked against the response. Then launches() and weather() were re-implemented in Python against the same payloads to watch the selection logic run.

open-meteo forecast       HTTP 200    4,526 bytes
open-meteo air quality    HTTP 200      354 bytes
launch library (list)     HTTP 200   25,334 bytes
launch library (detailed) HTTP 200   83,213 bytes

Bug 1 — the landing data was never there

fetch.h read results[0].rocket.launcher_stage[0].landing.location.abbrev. In the default list response rocket carries only configuration and id, so that path is absent and every launch would have fallen through to "booster downrange". It would not have crashed, which is worse — the LZ-4 sonic-boom warning would simply never have appeared.

mode=detailed supplies it: landing.location.abbrev is OCISLY with landing.type.abbrev ASDS for a droneship recovery. The band now distinguishes a droneship landing from an LZ-4 pad return, which is the one that arrives with booms.

That response is 83 kB. An ArduinoJson filter keeps only the four fields drawn:

83,213 bytes on the wire  ->  612 bytes held in the document

Bug 2 — the sky test read the wrong hour

The launch band was gated on nowCode <= 2, the weather code at the moment of the refresh. A dashboard drawn at 06:00 was deciding whether a 20:14 launch would be visible using the sky twelve hours earlier. Today's data shows how weak that proxy is: the daily code came back as 3, overcast, while the current code was 0, clear.

The request now carries hourly=cloud_cover, and the test reads cloud cover at the lift-off hour against WX_CLEAR_MAX_CLOUD_PCT — a threshold that had been defined in config.h since the design and never used.

Bug 3 — mission names truncated mid-word

Real names run to fifty characters. Falcon 9 Block 5 | SDA Tranche 1 Transport Layer A in a 40-byte field became FALCON 9 BLOCK 5 | SDA TRANCHE 1 TRANSP. The vehicle half is the least interesting part, so the name now reduces to its first two words and keeps the mission:

Falcon 9 Block 5 | USSF-153                        -> FALCON 9 - USSF-153
Falcon 9 Block 5 | SDA Tranche 1 Transport Layer A -> FALCON 9 - SDA TRANCHE 1 TRANSPORT LAYER A
Falcon 9 Block 5 | Transporter 18 (Dedicated SSO)  -> FALCON 9 - TRANSPORTER 18 (DEDICATED SSO RIDESHARE)

Anything still too long is cut at a word boundary. The renderer now measures the result at font 4 and steps down to font 2 rather than running into the lift-off time, and the PRIME tag is only drawn if it clears that column.

Still unexercised

The parsing is checked; the device is not. Panel output, Wi-Fi join, TLS on-chip, the battery ADC, the NVS write and deep-sleep wake have all still never run.

Sun 6 Sep 2026 · 16:05 PDT Firmware written and compilingBuilds Both sketches build against the real toolchain. Four things the compiler settled that the documentation did not, including a board profile that would have stranded 24 MB of flash.

Built, not just written

arduino-cli, esp32 core 3.3.11, ArduinoJson 7.4.3 and Seeed_GFX 2.0.3 are installed, and both sketches compile:

dashboard     1,145,691 bytes   9.1% of the 12 MB app0 slot
calibration     386,604 bytes
globals          49,188 bytes   15% of DRAM

Nothing has run on the device. Every runtime path — display output, Wi-Fi join, JSON parse, deep sleep — is still unexercised.

Structure

Network code and drawing code never meet. fetch.h fills a Model, render.h reads one, and neither knows the other exists.

FileRole
dashboard.inowake, connect, fetch, draw, sleep
model.hone struct holding everything the panel shows
layout.hcomputeLayout(), mirroring the mockup's function
render.hdraws the Model into the layout rectangles
fetch.hthe three keyless endpoints into the Model
battery.hpercentage from an interpolated discharge curve, runway fit stored in NVS

What the compiler settled

  • The six colors are TFT_*. Seeed's wiki lists GxEPD_* names; Seeed_GFX does not define them. Combo 521 sets USE_COLORFULL_EPAPER, which defines TFT_BLACK, TFT_WHITE, TFT_RED, TFT_YELLOW, TFT_BLUE and TFT_GREEN, and folds every other color name onto the nearest of them.
  • BOARD_SCREEN_COMBO belongs in driver.h. The library finds it through #if __has_include("driver.h"), so a define in the .ino reaches only the sketch and EPaper goes undefined at link time. Both sketches now carry one.
  • timegm() is absent from the ESP32's newlib. Launch times arrive from thespacedevs in UTC, and mktime() would apply the local zone to them. Replaced with an explicit days-from-civil conversion.
  • The board profile is wrong for this hardware. Seeed's wiki says select XIAO_ESP32S3, which offers only 8 MB of flash — this board carries 32 MB with two 12 MB OTA slots. Building that way writes an 8 MB partition table and strands 24 MB.

Flash layout preserved

build.sh targets esp32:esp32:esp32s3 with FlashSize=32M,PSRAM=opi,PartitionScheme=custom, and a partitions.csv beside each sketch reproduces the factory table read off the board. The build was checked rather than assumed: the generated table is the factory one, app0 is 12 MB, and the merged image is exactly 33,554,432 bytes.

Color calibration dropped

The firmware draws with the six symbolic constants, so the panel renders whatever those are and the mockup's hex values never reach it — calibrating them would have changed nothing downstream. Type legibility is the part that still has to be seen on the panel: whether font 1 labels and white-on-green survive at distance. That gets answered by the dashboard itself.

A gitignore hole, opened and closed

Copying secrets.h into the firmware sketch put it outside the anchored dashboard/secrets.h pattern, which matches only the directory beside the ignore file. In a repository that publishes to GitHub Pages that would have committed the Wi-Fi password. The pattern is now **/secrets.h, and git check-ignore confirms both copies are ignored while the example template stays tracked.

The constraint that decides everything

That removes menus, scrolling, live readouts, games, progress bars, and anything cursor-driven. Battery maths follows the same line. Three months assumes a six-hour refresh. A one-minute refresh would flatten it in days, so the refresh interval is the design decision, not an afterthought.

Fits the cadence — scheduled, wakes on a timer

  • Morning dashboard. Calendar, weather, tide or sunrise times, drawn once at 06:00 and once at 18:00. The canonical use, and the one the 3-month figure describes.
  • Role of the day from the AI learning guide. Pull one entry from ai-learning-guide/ each morning — company, role, what to learn for it. Turns a page nobody rereads into something passively encountered.
  • Dronscape capability of the day. Same pattern against the 60-feature landscape, one card per day with its source link as a QR code.
  • Workshop conditions log. SHT4x to microSD every few minutes; redraw a 24-hour temperature and humidity chart four times a day. The sensor is already on the board and currently does nothing.
  • Paper of the day. One arXiv abstract in the areas being tracked, fetched over Wi-Fi at a fixed hour.

Fits the cadence — on demand, a button press earns the wait

  • QR handoff to the phone or Quest. A button renders a QR for a chosen GitHub Pages demo URL. Testing already means opening a pushed page on a headset; a physical button that puts the link in front of the camera removes the typing. Twenty seconds is nothing against walking to a laptop.
  • Laser job preview. Render a pattern-tool SVG to the panel before cutting — six colors map onto pass or layer assignments, so the preview shows what each pass will do.
  • Dithered image frame. Six-color dithering against the Spectra palette is the same problem as the dithered QR idea already sitting in the pattern-tool list. The panel is the output device that makes it worth solving properly.
  • Bench sign. Green button cycles a status — cutting, do not enter, free — with the buzzer confirming the press. It holds the frame with the machine powered down.

Fits the hardware, not just the screen

  • Enclosure temperature alarm. SHT4x plus the buzzer, running on battery inside or beside the laser enclosure. The display only redraws when a threshold is crossed, so it costs almost nothing.
  • Home Assistant panel. ESPHome support is already there; this is the shortest path to something useful and a good way to learn the refresh behavior before writing firmware.

Sun 6 Sep 2026 · 12:28 PDT The CH340 link cannot sustain a long read — the ROM loader canFinding Backing up the 32 MB factory image failed three times. The stub flasher is twice as fast per byte and corrupts often enough to lose that advantage; esptool's slower --no-stub path passed every trial.

What prompted it

Backing up the factory image before flashing anything means reading all 32 MB off the board. A plain esptool read-flash 0x0 0x2000000 never finished: 921600 lost the chip the moment esptool switched rate, 460800 died with Invalid head of packet, and 115200 reached 240 kB before failing with Corrupt data, expected 0x1000 bytes but received 0xfea bytes.

Trials

Rather than spend another 50 minutes discovering a failure, characterise the link with 256 kB reads first. Every trial below is a read; nothing was written to the device.

ConfigShort reads passedThroughput
921600 + stub0 — loses the chip after the rate change—
460800 + stub0 — Invalid head of packet—
230400 + stub0 — short packets, expected 0x1000, received 0xfc7—
115200 + stub2 of 5≈10.8 kB/s while running
57600 + stub1 of 1, at 512 kB≈5.4 kB/s
115200 --no-stub4 of 4≈5.45 kB/s — 256 kB in 47–48 s

The inversion

esptool uploads a stub flasher by default because it moves data roughly twice as fast as the ROM loader. On this link that advantage disappears. At one success in two or three attempts, a 256 kB chunk costs about three tries, so the stub's effective throughput lands near 4 kB/s — below the ROM loader's steady 5.45 kB/s, and with far more babysitting.

Rule for this board: run everything at 115200, and pick the loader by address — the ROM loader below 16 MB, the stub flasher at and above it, where the ROM loader cannot reach. Below the line the usual advice, raise the baud and keep the stub, is backwards.

The failure is not a clean threshold. A single 256 kB read at 115200 with the stub passed on the first attempt, which is what made 115200 look reliable at first; repeating it showed 2 of 5. One sample was not enough to characterise a flaky link.

Working around it

seeed-studio-epaper/tools/read-flash-chunked.py reads the image 256 kB at a time, retries a chunk up to eight times, and keeps completed chunks on disk so an interrupted run resumes rather than restarting. It concatenates the parts at the end and prints the SHA-256. A corrupted chunk now costs 48 seconds instead of the whole transfer.

  --esptool .venv/bin/esptool \
  --port /dev/cu.usbserial-210 --chip esp32s3 \
  --baud 115200 --chunk 0x40000 --retries 10 \
  --out firmware-backup/2026-09-06_reterminal-e1002-factory-32MB.bin

Sun 6 Sep 2026 · 12:13 PDT reTerminal E1002 identified, ROM bootloader respondingResolved The board is a Seeed reTerminal E1002. Seeed routes its USB-C port through the CH340 to UART0 by design, and esptool reaches the ESP32-S3 ROM on the first attempt.

The device

The Seeed Studio reTerminal E1002 is a 7.3-inch E Ink Spectra 6 full-color ePaper terminal at 800×480, built on the ESP32-S3R8 with 8 MB PSRAM and 32 MB flash, Wi-Fi 4 and Bluetooth 5.0, a 2000 mAh battery, and USB-C 5 V/1 A input. Seeed's getting-started wiki and the Zephyr board documentation both cover it.

Why a CH340 sits in front of an ESP32-S3

The ESP32-S3 carries a native USB_SERIAL_JTAG peripheral, so a bridge chip looks redundant. Seeed wires one in deliberately: on the E1002 the USB port reaches UART0 through the CH340 rather than through USB_SERIAL_JTAG, which lets the CH340 drive the EN and BOOT pins and put the board into flashing mode over USB with no button press. The Zephyr board documentation states this directly.

So 1A86:7523 on this bench is the reTerminal E1002, and the generic USB Serial descriptor is expected rather than suspicious.

Talking to it

esptool v5.4.0, installed into a throwaway venv rather than the system Python, connects on the first attempt and drives its own reset sequence:

Silicon revision v0.2, 8 MB embedded PSRAM, and 32 MB of Winbond flash match the published E1002 specification exactly. e0:72:a1:f9:02:60 is the base MAC, which also identifies the board on Wi-Fi.

Why the earlier sweep heard nothing

  • The application firmware writes nothing to UART0, and the board spends most of its life asleep to reach its quoted three-month battery figure. There is no periodic console output to catch.
  • The ROM downloader answers esptool's SLIP-framed binary protocol, not human-readable text, so passive listening finds nothing legible at any baud rate.
  • The earlier reset attempt asserted DTR and RTS together and released them together. That is not the esptool download-mode sequence, so the chip came back up running its application rather than in the ROM downloader.
Corrected reading: the 36 stray bytes were noise, as suspected — but the UART was never dead. The link works; only the download-mode entry sequence was wrong.

  • Choose a path: Seeed's browser web-flasher, ESPHome for Home Assistant, Arduino, or Zephyr.

Sun 6 Sep 2026 · 12:07 PDT Identify the USB serial device on /dev/cu.usbserial-210Inconclusive A CH340 bridge enumerates cleanly, but nothing transmits on the UART across ten baud rates, reset pulses, and a 15-second passive listen.

Goal

Determine whether the device plugged into USB is a Seeed Studio board, and open a serial link to it.

What the host enumerates

ioreg reports a single USB serial device (the only other item on the bus is an Apple Watch charging cable):

/dev/cu.usbserial-210      (also /dev/tty.usbserial-210)
attached  Sep 6, 11:53
USB Product Name   "USB Serial"
idVendor           6790  = 0x1A86   QinHeng / WCH
idProduct         29987  = 0x7523   CH340 serial converter

The pair 1A86:7523 is the fixed identifier of the WCH CH340 USB-to-UART bridge — see DeviceHunt and the usb.ids registry. A CH340 is a bare bridge chip: it reports no board identity, only the generic string USB Serial, so the descriptors alone cannot name what sits behind it.

The bridge chip does not narrow the field much on its own. Seeed's XIAO family presents native USB under Seeed's own vendor ID 0x2886 — the Seeeduino XIAO enumerates as 2886:802F, per Seeed's driver INF — but many boards, Seeed's own included, route USB through a third-party UART bridge instead. A CH340 is equally consistent with a USB-to-TTL adapter, an Arduino clone, or a controller board.

Method

pyserial is absent from the Homebrew Python, so the probe drives the port through raw termios instead of adding a dependency. Three choices keep the probe non-destructive:

  • Open the callout node /dev/cu.*, which does not block waiting for carrier detect.
  • Clear HUPCL in the control flags, so closing the port does not drop DTR and reset an attached board.
  • Send only read-only queries: CR/LF, GRBL's ? status request and $I build-info request, and Marlin's M115 firmware-info request.

A second pass adds an Arduino-style auto-reset — assert DTR and RTS for 200 ms, release, then listen — and reaches non-standard baud rates through the macOS IOSSIOSPEED ioctl (0x80045402), which termios speed constants do not cover.

Reading the result

Three bursts totalling 36 bytes arrived across roughly thirty trials, at three unrelated baud rates, and none reproduced on a second attempt at the same rate. A genuine transmitter would produce framing errors at every wrong rate and clean text at the right one; it would also repeat. A floating UART RX pin picks up noise and emits exactly this pattern — occasional short random bursts, unrepeatable.

Conclusion: the CH340 bridge and its macOS driver work — the port opens, accepts baud changes, and drives DTR/RTS. Nothing is talking on the UART behind it.

Candidate causes

  • The adapter is a bare USB-to-TTL cable with nothing wired to its RX/TX pins.
  • The attached board is unpowered, held in reset, or runs firmware that prints nothing on boot.
  • TX is wired to TX and RX to RX rather than crossed.
  • Logic levels mismatch — a 5 V adapter against a 3.3 V board, or the reverse.

Next

  • Name the board, which narrows the expected baud and boot banner immediately.
  • Check for a power LED on the board and confirm the adapter's voltage-select jumper.
  • Confirm the crossover: adapter TX to board RX, adapter RX to board TX, grounds common.
  • If the board is an ESP8266 or ESP32, run esptool.py chip_id, which drives its own reset sequence and reports the chip over the ROM bootloader rather than relying on application output.
